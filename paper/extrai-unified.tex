\begin{abstract}
\noindent Language models are entering systematic-review workflows, but most evaluations use cloud models, cannot separate reading from training-data recall, and treat the human answer key as infallible. EXTRAI is a pre-registered, five-phase benchmark of five quantized open-weight models (8--14.8B parameters, one consumer mini-PC, integrated GPU, no cloud calls) --- plus a 27B build at 3.13 bits per weight, added as a pre-registered extension arm --- as meta-analysis workers over two published meta-analyses and their 21 trials, under two controls: a \emph{perturbation reading proof} (load-bearing numbers displaced under sealed maps, making recitation detectable) and a \emph{two-layer, source-verified answer key} that puts the human key on trial, documenting eleven source-confirmed discrepancies in one anchor and correcting its own cells twice. Reading: 168 perturbed sheets, zero attributable recitations, zero invention candidates. Arithmetic: 1 of 73 unaided confidence intervals exact, rising to 86\% through a trivial text-protocol calculator; pooling stayed beyond the cast and was closed, 12 of 12, only by the extension arm. Creation: \emph{all six} models' sheets reproduced the published dichotomous pools (morbidity 0.771--0.787 vs.\ 0.778), while the continuous diamond separated them: only \texttt{gemma4:12b} ($-0.27$) and the extension arm ($-0.25$ $[-0.33, -0.16]$) landed beside the published $-0.24$. Orchestration: under a warn-only ten-net harness, confirm-or-correct resolved every warning and the pooling call closed digit-consistent with the executed results. Deployment (clean texts, no answer key): the model sided with the sources at all seven discrepancies reaching a single extractable cell; its one failed tolerance decomposed to one mislabeled-dispersion cell. An instruction-language check (Portuguese drafts, identical materials) left every phase within replicate-level variance: no conclusion is an artifact of instruction language. All protocols, seals, sheets and raw outputs are public.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Systematic reviews and meta-analyses are the reference standard of clinical evidence synthesis and are labor-bound: registered reviews take 67 weeks on average from registration to publication \citep{borah2017time}, with extraction, checking and pooling consuming most of a team's hours. Large language models are entering exactly this workflow: feasibility studies report promising but uneven accuracy for trial data extraction \citep{schmidt2024exploring}; assisted extraction proved noninferior to human-only extraction in a live review \citep{lee2026noninferiority}; screening assistance is approaching routine use \citep{xie2026screening}; and end-to-end platforms now report complete reviews in hours \citep{bakker2026minutes}, with fully automated drafts rated above a published human-authored review by blinded specialists \citep{mclaughlin2026fully}. The major evidence-synthesis organizations jointly condition such use on demonstrated rigor, human oversight and transparent reporting \citep{flemyng2025position}.

Three gaps run through this literature. First, most evaluations use commercial cloud models, while clinical text pushes the other way: local open-weight models keep sensitive material on-premises and have shown extraction performance approaching expert annotation \citep{wiest2024privacy,spaanderman2025openweight}. Second, benchmarks that score agreement with a published review cannot distinguish a model that \emph{read} the primary trial from one that \emph{remembers} the review: the anchor and its primaries are plausibly in training corpora, and contamination inflates apparent competence \citep{xu2024contamination}. Third, the published review is usually treated as an infallible answer key, although hand-made evidence tables carry their own errors: of 27 meta-analyses using standardized mean differences, 17 (63\%) contained an extraction error in at least one of the two trials audited against the primary sources, some large enough to negate or reverse a finding \citep{gotzsche2007extraction}; and a later methodological review found the evidence base for established extraction standards weak despite that prevalence \citep{mathes2017frequency}. A benchmark that cannot notice when the published value is wrong will misgrade its best readers.

EXTRAI closes those three gaps at once: quantized open-weight models on a consumer machine with no cloud calls; a perturbation reading proof that makes recitation of published values detectable; and a two-layer, source-verified answer key whose quotation-backed rite puts the human key itself on trial. That verification produced a seventeen-entry record on one published anchor, eleven of them discrepancies confirmed against the primary sources, all of it in this study's public archive \citep{barra2026extrai}. Because the instruments were drafted in the team's working language before being frozen in English, the study also carries a built-in robustness check on instruction language.

This article reports a pre-registered, five-phase benchmark of local language models as meta-analysis workers, comparing five integrated-GPU models plus a sixth added after the campaign as a pre-registered extension arm, under contamination-controlled, source-verified grading against the published values.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Study design and pre-registration}
The study protocol --- phase order, model cast, hypotheses and compute budget --- was registered and committed to the public repository before any run; the two extensions executed within the deployment phase and the post-campaign extension arm that added a sixth model (Section~2.6) were each registered as a dated amendment before its own run. Corpora, sealed perturbations, answer keys, deterministic engines, graders and the detection harness were built and frozen before the campaign, during this study's own instrument-development phase.

The campaign ran five phases in an ascending line of investigation --- P1 \emph{Read}, P2 \emph{Calculate}, P3 \emph{Create}, P4 \emph{Orchestrate}, P5 \emph{Deploy} --- each specified below.

\subsection{Anchor meta-analyses and corpora}
Two recently published meta-analyses served as anchors. Anchor~1 addresses goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT) versus conventional fluid therapy in major surgery \citep{ashraf2026gdft}; its 14 primary randomized controlled trials constitute the first corpus (8 open-access from PubMed Central; 6 paywalled). It supplies three dichotomous outcome families (overall morbidity, mortality, postoperative ileus) and two continuous ones. The anchor's own outcome tables roster 4, 2 and 3 of the 14 trials for morbidity, mortality and ileus respectively, and the 14 meet together only in the anchor's descriptive-characteristics and risk-of-bias tables. All 14 were extracted here, each contributing to whichever outcome families it reports, and the extraction-level analyses span all 14. Anchor~2 addresses low-carbohydrate diets and glycemic control (HbA1c) in type~2 diabetes \citep{khraise2026lowcarb}; its 7 primary trials constitute the second corpus, and all 7 report its single continuous outcome (change in HbA1c, \%), whose published random-effects pooled estimate, $-0.24$ $[-0.32, -0.16]$, is this study's external reference value.

\subsection{The perturbation reading proof}
Before any model reads a trial, a sealed, deterministic operator displaces load-bearing numbers in its text. A model that returns a displaced value read the text in front of it; one that returns the original published value is reciting training data \citep{xu2024contamination}. Models operate exclusively in the perturbed world; the original$\leftrightarrow$perturbed maps are held by the graders alone and applied only after extraction, as a pure table lookup (the ``unperturbation lens''). The sealed maps were reused unchanged across all phases, their SHA-256 fingerprints printed into each phase's run log. Phase P5 deliberately runs on clean texts and therefore makes no reading-proof claim (Section~\ref{sec:p5}).

\subsection{Answer keys and the adjudication rite}
Each corpus is graded against a two-layer, source-verified answer key: layer~1 holds the anchor's published cells; layer~2 holds each cell verified against the primary's full text, with a literal supporting quotation and a per-cell verdict. The key itself is on trial by design: source verification of Anchor~1 produced a public, quotation-backed record of source-confirmed discrepancies in the published meta-analysis (Table~\ref{tab:errata}), and the campaign graded against the key as corrected through the most recent adjudications. Anchor~2's verification found no discrepancies: its statistics reproduced cleanly.

Mechanical grading is backed by an adjudication rite: no cell changes category without the source quotation that decides it, and the adjudicators' own errors are published in the same record as everyone else's. The rite exists because judges exhibit a measurable \emph{plausibility bias}: during key construction the graded record reversed this study's own human--AI adjudicator six times, twice withdrawing accusations of model fabrication in which the models had extracted literally correct values that rigid verification windows had hidden \citep{barra2026extrai}. Quotation-before-verdict is the structural constraint on that bias, for human, hybrid and model judges alike. That adjudication layer is itself the hybrid --- the author working with a cloud-based frontier assistant (disclosed in the Declarations) --- so the study's local-only property is scoped to the \emph{evaluated pipelines} (Section~2.6), never to grading and review.

\subsection{Instruments}
All model-facing instruments are frozen English prompts, committed to the study's record at registration: the structured extraction sheets, the arithmetic tasks, the calculator protocol, the harness dialogue, and the trigger and synthesis prompts. The instruments were originally drafted in Brazilian Portuguese, the research team's working language, and translated field-by-field under a published correspondence table (for example, \texttt{ic95\_md}$\rightarrow$\texttt{ci95\_md}; \texttt{RESULTADO}$\rightarrow$\texttt{RESULT}); the originals are archived, and Section~\ref{sec:lang} describes the robustness analysis this enables.

\subsection{Models and hardware}
Five quantized open-weight models sized for an integrated GPU formed the comparison cast: \texttt{gemma4:12b}, \texttt{qwen3:14b}, \texttt{llama3.1:8b}, \texttt{qwen3.5:9b} and \texttt{deepseek-r1:14b}. All phases ran on one consumer mini-PC (AMD Ryzen~7, Radeon~780M integrated GPU, 32~GB RAM, no discrete GPU), served by Ollama with a 16{,}384-token context, reasoning channels disabled, and a uniform 4{,}000-token output allowance for extraction tasks; no cloud API was called in any evaluated pipeline (the grading and adjudication layer is cloud-assisted; Section~2.4). One model was resident at a time, with an explicit memory unload between models. Phase P4 used only \texttt{gemma4:12b}, pre-designated in the protocol as the orchestration candidate on the evidence of this study's instrument-development runs.

After the campaign closed, a sixth model entered as a pre-registered extension arm (protocol Amendment~1, dated 2026-09-07 and committed before its run): \texttt{smtek/Qwen3.8-27B:Q2\_K\_XL}, an openly published mixed-precision quantization of a 27.3-billion-parameter model --- the reader the author had meanwhile adopted as primary extractor in a separate systematic-review harness, and the reason for the arm. Its two printed names disagree and neither describes its weights (the tag reads \texttt{Q2\_K\_XL}; the GGUF \texttt{file\_type} header, which is what the runtime prints, reads \texttt{Q4\_K\_S}), so the build was characterized by reading the tensor table of the weight file itself: 27.32~billion parameters in 10.68~GB at \textbf{3.13 bits per weight}, mixed across six ggml types (IQ3\_XXS on 77.4\% of parameters, IQ3\_S on 11.7\%, Q3\_K and Q2\_K on 4.7\% each, IQ4\_XS on 1.5\%, IQ1\_M on 0.1\%). The cast's five builds are Q4\_K\_M, about 4.5 bits per weight; resident footprints were 4.9--9.3~GB for the cast and 10.7~GB for the extension arm. It ran phases P1--P3 under the identical instruments, seals, keys, engines and graders, called exactly like the cast (same context, reasoning off, same output allowance, stock chat template), 54 calls in 119 minutes. After the graders were re-run over the extended cast, the five-model record was verified unchanged field by field.

\subsection{Phase P1 --- structured extraction}
Each of the five models, and later the extension arm, extracted all 14 perturbed Anchor-1 primaries from zero, twice (two independent replicates), under the frozen English extraction sheet: 30 fields per trial, each an object \texttt{\{"value", "where"\}}, the mandatory \texttt{where} being the model-side provenance instrument --- a locator naming the section or table the value came from, not a verbatim quotation. Grading used the eligible cell set of the two-layer key --- cells with a usable, digit-bearing source-verified value, 124 per model --- comparing the seal-reversed first-parseable sheet against the key's source layer with a magnitude-based numeric comparator. That comparator is a declared approximation of the full ruler, so every non-matching cell was listed for adjudication rather than auto-verdicted. Replicate stability applied the same comparator between the two raw replicates. Recitation candidates were detected mechanically --- a cell whose raw text contains a seal pair's original value while the displaced image the model actually read is absent --- and each was adjudicated against the perturbed source text before any verdict. Divergent cells were classified by factuality (Table~\ref{tab:p1} defines the three classes) as \emph{omission}, \emph{invention candidate} --- a number absent from the perturbed text and not derivable from it by percentage, sum, difference or mean --- or \emph{value/format divergence}; the same invention screen ran over every cell of the Anchor-2 sheets of phase P3, both replicates. Each non-matching cell was then re-examined against the perturbed text its model read and classified by mechanism, with residues adjudicated under the rite; the cell-by-cell record is Supplementary Tables~S1--S7.

\subsection{Phase P2 --- meta-analytic arithmetic}
Each model computed, over its own P1 sheets, per-study risk ratios and mean differences with 95\% confidence intervals, and pooled estimates, in two arms: arm~A unaided, and arm~B through a plain-text calculator protocol in which the model writes lines of the form \texttt{CALC: function(arguments)}, the harness executes the validated function and returns \texttt{RESULT:} into the context (up to 5 rounds and 20 calls), and the model then answers with a final JSON. Three prompt families (risk ratios, mean differences, pools) $\times$ two arms $\times$ two replicates ran per model. Grading recomputed the truth with the same validated engine from the input block the model saw, under pre-registered parsing rules: event counts read from forms such as ``19 (32.8\%)''; percent-only cells converted against the group size; means accepted only as mean~$\pm$~SD, so median (IQR) cells have no computable truth and \emph{not computable} is correct there. Each quantity was labeled exact, right-direction, wrong, correctly-refused, wrongly-refused, or unsupported; intervals and pooled estimates were graded separately.

\subsection{Phase P3 --- deterministic creation of both meta-analyses}
Everything downstream of the sheets was executed by pre-registered, frozen code; no model touched a number after extraction. For Anchor~1, the engines parsed each model's P1 sheets, selected event/denominator routes per outcome, computed per-study risk ratios, pooled them under both Mantel--Haenszel and DerSimonian--Laird \citep{dersimonian1986meta}, applied the sealed reversal, and classified every difference from the published tables into a frozen five-category comparison (\emph{reproduces} / \emph{differs by a documented anchor discrepancy} / \emph{model route} / \emph{model error} / \emph{source unavailable}). The pooled comparison uses DerSimonian--Laird because the published pooled numbers instantiate that method under a Mantel--Haenszel caption (documented discrepancy \#15). For Anchor~2, each model freshly extracted the 7 perturbed primaries (two replicates) under the English continuous-outcome sheet, which, unlike the Anchor-1 sheet, records bare values with no provenance field --- an instrument asymmetry carried over from this study's instrument-development draft of the same sheet. A deterministic route selector then built each trial's sextet (mean, SD, n per arm) by the frozen rules: declared change used directly; standard errors and confidence intervals converted to SDs; baseline-and-final pairs combined under the Cochrane $r=0.5$ rule \citep{higgins2023handbook}. The DerSimonian--Laird pool was then computed twice: in the perturbed world, and through the graders' sealed lens for comparison with the published $-0.24$ $[-0.32, -0.16]$. Tolerances were pre-registered at $\pm0.01$ for risk ratios and interval bounds and $\pm0.1$ for mean differences.

\subsection{Phase P4 --- orchestration under a warn-only detection harness}
The study's ten-net detection harness follows the doctrine that nets \emph{detect and warn, never substitute a value}. The doctrine is empirical: in this study's instrument-development testing, checking stages given write-power did net harm through the same plausibility bias --- an auditing model ``repaired'' correct cells by imputing plausible values (once correcting a sample size to 24.5 while quoting, in its own verdict, the text's ``n = 28''), a calculator stage flipped the sign of a control arm that had genuinely worsened ($+0.3$ written as $-0.3$), and a pipeline's quality gate did six times more damage to the pooled estimate than the planted sabotage it caught \citep{barra2026extrai}. The harness comprises schema-constrained typed calls (\texttt{\{"function", "arguments", "source", "derivation"\}}), an arity check with the full function signature, a sign-echo net, an arm-agnostic declared-source check, a declared-derivation check, an argument type system with per-class value impossibilities, a confidence-interval bound-order check, a coherence check tying each interval call to the study's own last executed mean difference, a negative-SD result warning, a closing-versus-executed check, per-call warning budgets with confirm-by-repeat, and an escalation flag (\texttt{requires\_human\_review}) on insistence. \texttt{gemma4:12b} then ran one formal pipeline over its own Anchor-2 sheets: per-study typed calls, a model-emitted DerSimonian--Laird pooling call over its own sextets, a product-layer coherence check on the reported intervals (flagged, never endorsed), and a narrative synthesis checked mechanically for orphan numbers. No model stage saw an answer key, a published value, or the seal; grader-side comparison happened only afterward.

\subsection{Phase P5 --- deployment on clean texts}\label{sec:p5}
The deployment phase ran the same architecture on the \emph{original, unperturbed} texts of both anchors with no answer key in any loop --- the day-to-day usage scenario. Its components: (i) fresh two-replicate extraction of all 21 primaries by \texttt{gemma4:12b} under the English instruments; (ii) a frozen discrepancy-cell panel --- the seven of Anchor~1's eleven source-confirmed discrepancies that resolve to a single extractable cell with a decided source value --- testing whether the model's cell sides with the source or with the anchor's published value; (iii) deterministic recomputation of both meta-analyses and per-trial three-way tables (source quotation $\times$ the anchor authors' cell $\times$ the model's cell); and (iv) a three-configuration comparison of error detection over the same sheets: the deterministic engine alone, the engine plus deterministic sheet-versus-source nets, and the model orchestrating under the conversational ten-net harness (Table~\ref{tab:configs}).

\subsection{Comparison conventions and software}
All computation used Python~3.12 with the repository's validated engine functions (risk ratio and its log-scale interval; mean difference and its interval; SD conversions; Mantel--Haenszel, DerSimonian--Laird and inverse-variance pooling); the risk-ratio function reproduces a published per-study value of Anchor~1 as its standing test case. No external statistics package was used. Replicates measure stability, not statistical significance; with one corpus per anchor the model comparisons are descriptive. Compute totaled 277 model calls across P1--P4 ($\approx$10.8~h of machine time) plus 54 calls ($\approx$2.0~h) for the extension arm, every queue resume-safe.

\subsection{Robustness analysis: instruction language}\label{sec:lang}
This study's instrument-development phase had exercised the identical corpora, seals, keys, engines and graders under the Portuguese drafts of the instruments (Section~2.5), so that archived record provides a natural comparison arm in which instruction language is the only changed variable. For each phase, the directly comparable quantities of the two records were compared, under the pre-registered acceptance criterion that differences stay within replicate-level variance.

\section{Results}

\subsection{P1 --- extraction: the reading proof held, and no attributable recitation occurred}
All 140 extraction calls (5 models $\times$ 14 trials $\times$ 2 replicates) completed with clean stops in 390.4 minutes; every sheet parsed at the sheet level except one replicate (\texttt{deepseek-r1:14b}, one trial), whose pair served. Cell agreement with the corrected two-layer key --- after the four grader-artifact adjudications (Table~\ref{tab:p1} caption) --- ranged from 89/124 (71.8\%) to 105/124 (84.7\%), and replicate stability from 77.4\% to 99.2\% (Table~\ref{tab:p1}). The extension arm's 28 sheets completed with clean stops in 77.9 minutes and all parsed; graded identically, it scored 108/124 (87.1\%) --- the highest of the six (Table~\ref{tab:p1}, last row). Because the numeric comparator cannot see format or granularity equivalence, cross-model ranking by this score alone is provisional; the value-level separation appears in P3.

The mechanical recitation detector raised 5 candidate cells across the cast's 140 sheets, and adjudication against the perturbed source texts resolved all 5 to incomplete-perturbation artifacts rather than recall: three models independently wrote a displaced count (113) that is arithmetically reconstructible from a surviving percentage printed beside it in the perturbed text (57.7\% $\times$ n=196 = 113.1), and two models transcribed a value (4088) that survives verbatim in a prose restatement the perturbation operator missed. Zero attributable recitations remained. The same three models copied, rather than reconstructed, the equally inconsistent control-arm cell of the same table --- an asymmetric preference shared across architectures, recorded as a behavior note.

The factuality classification of the divergent cells (Table~\ref{tab:p1}, middle columns) found \textbf{zero invention candidates in every model}, the extension arm included. Most divergences were themselves replicate-stable: 17/21 for \texttt{gemma4:12b}, 20/20 for \texttt{qwen3:14b}, 13/20 for \texttt{qwen3.5:9b}, 16/26 for \texttt{deepseek-r1:14b}, but only 17/36 for \texttt{llama3.1:8b}, and 16/17 for the extension arm (counted over the comparator's raw divergent inventories, before the four-cell adjudication; \texttt{deepseek-r1:14b}'s denominator excludes the divergent cell in its unparseable-replicate trial).

Supplementary Tables~S1--S7 (after the references) account cell by cell for the 124 comparator failures across the five models --- the divergences behind these counts, not a per-model total --- each re-examined against the perturbed text its model read and classified by mechanism: 12 omissions; 27 equivalent re-encodings; 26 population-layer choices; 34 case-mix summaries; 12 genuine reading slips; 9 unmatched ratio-style re-expressions; and 4 cells adjudicated \emph{in the models' favor} as a grader-side lookup collision (Table~S7). The extension arm's 17 divergent cells fall into the same mechanisms: the seven case-mix summaries every reader produces, the two FEDORA population-layer cells, one re-encoding (\texttt{0 (0)} for \texttt{0 (0\%)}), six omissions and the same Castro collision cell.

\section{Discussion}

This study measured, in one continuous pre-registered chain, what small local models running on an integrated GPU can contribute to meta-analysis --- and where the risk concentrates. Under the perturbation control, none of these results is attributable to memory of the published review.

The division of difficulty appears structural as much as cognitive. Dichotomous material is transcription --- counts and denominators printed redundantly across table, prose and percentage --- and the pipeline \emph{dampens} what error remains: a risk ratio is a ratio, so a different population layer shifts numerator and denominator together, and the weights derive from the counts themselves. Table~\ref{tab:p3ma1} shows it: six models took visibly different denominator routes and still landed within a hundredth of the published morbidity pool. Each continuous quantity, by contrast, is printed once, in one of several competing layers, and the decisive step is semantic --- identifying what a printed dispersion \emph{is}. A mislabeled type sends the conversion down the wrong route, and inverse-variance pooling then \emph{amplifies} the error, because weight scales with 1/SD\textsuperscript{2} \citep{higgins2023handbook}: one dispersion four-fold too small dominated a pool and inflated its heterogeneity in the deployment phase. The same architecture is thus error-dampening for count outcomes and error-amplifying for continuous ones, which gives the promising-but-uneven extraction accuracy of the feasibility literature \citep{schmidt2024exploring} a structure: in this record the unevenness tracks the outcome type at least as much as the model.

Nothing in the chain corrects a divergent cell: the downstream is deterministic, the nets only warn, the graders only classify, and the unperturbation lens is a pure table lookup. Table~\ref{tab:p3ma2}'s before-reversal column shows that neutrality mechanically: the four distant diamonds passed through the lens and stayed distant. The pools still match the published ones because the divergent cells of Table~\ref{tab:p1} concentrate in baseline descriptor fields the engines never consume, and in population-layer choices the ratio structure dampens. The six diamonds spread widely through reading-class differences alone: one engine computed all six. Once everything deterministic is code, reading is the whole game, and model choice is a reading choice. The extension arm sharpens what \emph{well-chosen} means: at a comparable footprint it carried roughly twice the parameters at about seven-tenths the precision (Section~2.6) and read better than every model in the cast on both anchors. In this record the parameter count, not the bit depth, tracked reading quality --- a lead for the next campaign rather than a result of this one. Even the cast's best diamond, \texttt{gemma4:12b}'s, falls short of the anchor's homogeneity (Table~\ref{tab:p3ma2}) --- the same effect, direction and significance over a noisier reconstructed evidence body, the residue plausibly injected by route-level differences (population layers, dispersion conversions) rather than by any single wrong cell --- while the extension arm's I\textsuperscript{2} shows that homogeneity to be reachable by reading alone. ``Beside the published value'' is therefore a claim about the estimate, not digit-level equivalence of the whole meta-analysis; the wider interval is what a practitioner would inherit.

None of those reading failures was a fabrication: the invention screen found no cell whose numbers cannot be traced to the text read, and the inventory --- omissions, population-layer choices, format variants, mislabeled quantities, replicate-stable in the stronger readers --- is systematic and auditable, not stochastic. That profile is what makes a warn-only architecture viable: nets suffice for traceable errors, whereas fabricating models would demand the value-substituting interventions the doctrine forbids. A cloud-model extraction study reports the same shape --- across 160 data items its errors were few and \emph{missed data was the most common error type} \citep{gartlehner2024extraction} --- so omission dominating fabrication at opposite ends of the size and deployment spectrum suggests a property of the extraction task, not of one model class. The pooled-arithmetic ceiling agrees: even with the calculator, the list-valued pooling calls stayed beyond the whole 8--14.8B cast --- which is why the architecture moves pooling into code rather than relying on the reader's size. Hence the triage: dichotomous evidence tables may be delegated broadly; continuous outcomes concentrate the model risk and deserve the strongest reader and the strongest checks.

The deployment phase turns the benchmark toward real use, where no answer key exists. At all seven cells of the frozen discrepancy panel the model sided with the \emph{source} against the published value (the other four of the eleven fall outside it: a method-caption error, a sample-size convention spanning six trials, and two cells whose sources report nothing verifiable to side with; Table~\ref{tab:errata}). A local reader plus a three-way table makes such discrepancies \emph{visible} instead of requiring trust in either extractor. One carries conclusion-level weight (Table~\ref{tab:errata}, \#16): the anchor's significant ileus reduction (abstract RR 0.48) rests on the mislabeled pulmonary-complication row, and over the two genuinely-ileus trials the pool crosses 1 under either method; its no-difference conclusions for morbidity and mortality survive every documented discrepancy, as the six-model reproductions demonstrate. This is consistent with reports that model assistance can match human extraction quality in live reviews \citep{lee2026noninferiority}, and adds that disagreement informs in both directions: the same source-verification process corrected this study's own key twice during development. 

The phase's one failed tolerance decomposed to a single cell, where the source prints the same quantity twice --- a table interval and a prose ``$\pm$'' --- and the model transcribed one layer under the wrong label. That ambiguity in the source's own notation seeds the defect, which defines this architecture's residual failure class: a model that misdeclares its sheet escapes every check that trusts the sheet. The class is not peculiar to machine readers. An audit of a published sports-medicine meta-analysis found the same dispersion mix-up in 9 of its 16 effect sizes; correcting it moved the pooled estimate from a standardized mean difference of 1.46 [0.91, 2.01] to 0.54 [0.37, 0.71] and cost a second comparison its significance, over a trial literature in which nearly one report in five describes variability with a standard error rather than a standard deviation \citep{sandercock2024mixup}. The class therefore deserves a dedicated sheet-versus-source dispersion net rather than a better model, in any pipeline, human or machine. The instrument asymmetry likely mattered: the continuous sheet carries no provenance field, whereas the dichotomous sheet's mandatory \texttt{where} locator repeatedly disambiguated population layers during adjudication; extending provenance to every sheet is the corresponding instrument work, never retroactive. 

Detection layers are complements, not substitutes. Conversational nets catch conversation-layer defects but are structurally blind to a well-formed poisoned cell, which only the sheet-versus-source net saw; the weight-share product flag was blind too, because random-effects weighting re-equalizes shares. P4 supplies the complementary case: confirm-or-correct resolved every warning that fired --- the net states what it saw, the model confirms or revises, no value is ever overwritten --- after which the model's own pooling call closed digit-consistent with its executed results. A net that can only point still changes outcomes, provided a model is there to answer it. No warn-only configuration made the wrong number right --- by doctrine it must not --- so the safest key-free architecture this record supports is a deterministic engine, sheet-versus-source nets, a conversational harness for orchestration slips, and a reviewer dispatched to the primary's table whenever any flag fires.

Unlike most of the literature it joins, everything measured here runs locally, at zero marginal cost, on hardware that keeps clinical text on-premises; the perturbation proof, the public seals and the adjudication rite operationalize the conditions the evidence-synthesis organizations attach to such use \citep{flemyng2025position}. That reading-versus-recall control matters more, not less, as anchors and primaries enter training corpora \citep{xu2024contamination}, and the two operator gaps behind this campaign's five mechanical candidates are now demonstrated across architectures and queued as instrument fixes.

These discrepancies also measure the human side of extraction, and the two extractor kinds fail differently --- with one telling overlap. That the human column carries errors at all is consistent with the methodological literature rather than remarkable within it \citep{gotzsche2007extraction,mathes2017frequency}. What this study adds is the cell-by-cell source-quotation trail and a model column audited beside the human one. The anchor's eleven source-confirmed discrepancies (Table~\ref{tab:errata}) are transcription and labeling slips, and, like the models, the human extractors fabricated nothing; the model profile concentrates instead in omissions, layer choices and format variants. The overlap is the instructive part: the one human error with conclusion-level weight and the one model error that distorted a pool are the \emph{same class}, a mislabel --- an outcome heading, a dispersion type --- suggesting that label semantics, not numbers, is where extraction fails for both kinds of extractor, and therefore where verification effort should concentrate.

Plausibility bias --- any judge's tendency, human, hybrid or model, to prefer a plausible reading over the printed one --- runs through the instruments and the findings alike. It shaped the instruments before a single campaign run: both the adjudication rite and the warn-only doctrine exist because of it (Sections~2.4 and~2.10). In the campaign the bias was measured, not presumed, and appeared exactly once --- on the judge's side: the deployment record initially charged the model with \emph{computing} the mislabeled dispersion, until a detection-net run showed the value printed verbatim in the source's own prose and the charge was corrected with a dated note in the archived record \citep{barra2026extrai}. The same containment caught the grading layer again, mechanically rather than judgmentally, in the four verbatim-correct transcriptions that a seal-pair lookup collision had charged as divergences and that were adjudicated in the models' favor (Supplementary Table~S7). The model stages showed zero occurrences: no value was ever substituted (structurally forbidden), the sign-echo net never fired, and all six transcribed even the sign-sensitive cells verbatim --- a factuality profile opposite to the judges'. This aligns with documented model-judge biases \citep{zheng2023judging}, which appear to generalize beyond model judges: the constraint that works is structural --- quote before verdict, flag before fix --- not a better judge.

The instruction-language robustness check closes a confounder rather than opening a finding. A referee could ask whether drafting the instruments in Portuguese and freezing them in English handicapped --- or peculiarly helped --- models trained predominantly on English; per phase the two records agree within replicate-level variance (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). This suggests that multilingual instruction-following is effectively solved at these model sizes for structured tasks, and establishes that no conclusion above is an artifact of instruction language.

Several limitations bound these claims. Two anchors from a single journal family and 21 primaries are the whole evidence base; every superlative is scoped to these corpora, and no formal between-model inference is attempted. One quantization per model and one hardware profile were tested, so precision and parameter count are not separable here: the extension arm was added after the campaign, run once, and not taken through P4, and its advantage is measured against five builds that differ from it in both. The mechanical cell comparator is a declared approximation of the full adjudicated ruler, so absolute cell scores understate agreement and cross-model gaps partly reflect format habits. The perturbation operator's residual gaps (derivable totals; prose restatements) are documented and exploitable, though they produce reconstruction, not recall. The English harness build reproduces the frozen net logic but was exercised in one formal run of one model. The adjudicator is the same human--AI pair that built the harness, mitigated by mandatory quotation and public self-correction rather than independence, and the known biases of model judges \citep{zheng2023judging} are constrained, not eliminated, by that rite. Finally, the deployment phase's clean texts cannot separate reading from recall by design; its claims rest on the perturbed phases' proof.

In conclusion, a consumer machine running one well-chosen local reader --- a 12-billion-parameter model at 4.5 bits per weight, or, at the same per-call latency and a comparable footprint, 27 billion parameters at 3.13 --- a frozen deterministic engine and warn-only detection nets can rebuild published meta-analyses to the published digits, expose documented errors in them with source quotations, and fail in ways that are named, single-celled and flaggable --- and none of this depends on the instruction language. The designed next step is the generalization gate: the same frozen architecture against a new anchor in a new clinical domain, where transfer, not replication, is the question.
